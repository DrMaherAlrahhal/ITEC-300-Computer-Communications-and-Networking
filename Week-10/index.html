🎯 Course Learning Outcomes

CLO 3 (Official): Apply principles of computing to identify the basic mechanisms, services and protocols for the organizational network infrastructure.

CLO 4 (Official): Work in a team to evaluate the devices and protocols to design a network using suitable explanations for the given problem.

Week 10 learning goals: Recognize security risks, distinguish network and data protection, select appropriate safeguards, and justify a security design for an organization.

Learning path: Identify a threat → Understand what can be harmed → Choose protection → Test the design.

01 · What Can Go Wrong?

A network connects valuable information and devices. A threat is something that could cause harm; a vulnerability is a weakness; an attack is an attempt to exploit it.

Threat

Someone tries to steal student records.

Vulnerability

An account uses a weak password.

Control

MFA reduces the risk of stolen-password access.

02 · CIA Triad: What Are We Protecting?

Confidentiality means only authorized people can read data. Integrity means data remains correct. Availability means systems are usable when needed.

Choose one of the three security goals.
Quick challenge: A student grade was changed without permission. Which CIA goal was violated?

04 · Authentication vs Authorization

Authentication: Who are you? Authorization: What are you allowed to access?

🔐 Defense toolkit

MFA: More than one verification factor.

Least privilege: Give only the access needed.

RBAC: Permissions depend on a defined role.

Audit logs: Record activity for investigation.

Illustrative classroom policy; real university permissions may differ.

05 · Protect Data: Encryption and Hashing

Encryption makes information unreadable without an appropriate key. Hashing produces a one-way fingerprint useful for integrity checking. They are not the same operation.

🔑 Toy encryption demo

Try changing the secret shift value. This classical Caesar cipher is for learning only—not real security.

📦 What protects data?

Choose a data protection situation.
Important: Real networks use modern cryptographic protocols such as TLS and properly managed encryption keys. Do not use the toy cipher to secure actual information.
